# Short video and slideshows

Sources: Oliver Henry's slideshow guide (Snugly), Beto's Inkigo, Pushcroll's founders, a Playkit report on TripBFF (read 2026-11-14 as articles; all view and revenue numbers are the creators' own).

## The hook that works
**Another person + a conflict -> I showed them -> they changed their mind.**
Henry's hooks in this shape got 147K-234K views; hooks about himself or about a feature got 0.9K-2.7K.
Toogo versions (write them in Hebrew first):
- "She wanted the beach, I wanted museums. We both swiped yes on this."
- "Four friends, one trip, zero arguments in the group chat."
- "My husband said planning Japan would take weeks. I sent him one link."
Never open with the app's name or a feature.

## Photo slideshow (cheapest format)
6 portrait slides: (1) the hook over a strong photo, (2-5) the plan unfolding - places, the match, the day - (6) a soft call to action ("the app is Toogo"). Only photos that pass `toogo-rules` 5. Slides can be built from the cards table by script, but a person picks the photos.

## Reel format (TripBFF)
A question as the hook, friends' own phone footage, a short app demo on the beat drop. They post ~3 a day and re-post the same reel with new audio and a new cover.

## Rhythm and expectations
- TikTok first. Instagram did worse for the small apps looked at (one averaged ~20 views a reel).
- Expect a long flat start: Pushcroll had ~6 months of nothing before one video hit.
- Judge a hook after 3-5 posts, not one.

## Do not
- Phone farms, comment bots, fake accounts (against the platforms' terms).
- Promise features that are not live (`toogo-rules` 6), or show weak photos.
- Paid influencer deals before organic works.

Deliverable: for each idea, the hook line (Hebrew and English), the 6 slides or shot list, the caption, and which Toogo screen is shown.
